## Challenge — Compare diabetes features

The [executed notebook](notebook.ipynb) compares BMI (`bmi`) with average blood pressure (`bp`) as separate inputs to a linear regression model predicting diabetes progression one year after baseline. Both use exactly the same train/test rows with `test_size=0.33` and `random_state=42`. BMI predicts better on this split: test R² is **0.3164** and RMSE is **62.7221**, compared with blood pressure's R² of **0.1817** and RMSE of **68.6275**. The two labeled plots show test observations with lines fitted only to training data. BMI's higher R² and lower RMSE still leave substantial variation unexplained; a single-feature association does not demonstrate a cause or a treatment effect.

Weight and waist have negative slopes for all three exercise measures, whereas pulse associations are weak. The fitted lines and in-sample R²/RMSE describe associations, rather than performance on unseen people. Linnerud has only **20 rows** from middle-aged men in one fitness club: this **small sample** makes the work **exploratory** and **non-causal**. Differences between participants and other unmeasured factors could explain the patterns, so the plots cannot show that doing more situps causes a change in waist or that these associations generalize to other populations.
