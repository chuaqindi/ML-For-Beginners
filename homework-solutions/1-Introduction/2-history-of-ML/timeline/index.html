A short, sourced history

From rules to learning

Machine learning grew through an exchange between mathematics, psychology, engineering, public funding, and steadily more capable computers.

How to read this timeline

No single discovery created modern ML. Each milestone opened a possibility, exposed a limit, or changed which experiments researchers could afford to run.

1943

A mathematical neuron

Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts described a simplified computational model of a neuron, connecting logic with ideas about biological cognition.

Source: timeline of machine learning

1950

Can machines think?

Alan Turing reframed an abstract philosophical dispute as an observable imitation game, now commonly called the Turing Test.

Source: the Turing Test

1956

Artificial intelligence gets a name

The Dartmouth workshop gathered researchers around the proposal that aspects of intelligence could be described precisely enough for machines to simulate.

Dartmouth's historical account

1957

The perceptron learns a boundary

Frank Rosenblatt's perceptron demonstrated how a machine could adjust weights from examples to form a linear decision boundary.

Perceptron overview

1966

ELIZA mirrors conversation

Joseph Weizenbaum's program used pattern matching to produce therapist-like dialogue, revealing both the power and fragility of apparent understanding.

Source: history of artificial intelligence

1974–1980

The first AI winter

Funding and enthusiasm contracted when systems failed to meet large promises and remained constrained by limited data and computing power.

Course discussion of the AI winter

1980s

Expert systems find business uses

Rule-based systems encoded specialist knowledge for narrow domains, producing commercial success while proving expensive to maintain.

Expert systems overview

1997

Deep Blue defeats Kasparov

IBM's chess system combined specialized hardware, search, evaluation rules, and expert knowledge to defeat the reigning world champion.

Source: history of artificial intelligence

2012

Deep learning changes computer vision

AlexNet's ImageNet performance showed how neural networks, large datasets, and GPU computation could substantially improve image classification.

Source: timeline of machine learning
